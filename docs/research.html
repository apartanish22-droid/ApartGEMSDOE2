Measured historical geometry

What the GEMSDOE32 file actually changed

The historical GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 dot masks are identical. GEMSDOE32 contains all but 6,436 of GEMSDOE25's dots and all but 2,545 of GEMSDOE31's. GEMSDOE36 contains every GEMSDOE32 dot plus 1,200. These are independent pixel counts on pinned files, not proof of how they were generated or of their organizer scores.

The metric rewards proximity to true fault pixels while charging for predicted mass far from truth. Thinning can reduce redundancy and false-positive cost. That offers a plausible explanation of the owner-reported ranking, not a causal demonstration; the true hidden labels and file-bound receipts are unavailable. A future method could do better, but none has been shown to do so here.

01 / Highest-priority question · medium–high cost

Magnetic contact-offset consistency

Layers and signature: competition total magnetic intensity and reduced-to-pole magnetics; trace coherent anomaly textures on opposite sides of a candidate boundary and measure a spatially consistent tangential offset rather than raw gradient magnitude alone.

Why it may find a missing fault: fault displacement of magnetic rock units may remain visible through cover even where the surface trace is not catalogued. Non-fault mimics: igneous contacts, varying lithology and flight-line processing artifacts. This repo has never implemented it; peer edge/persistence work is related, so cross-repository novelty is not established. Data bands are described officially, but the cache is absent and the lane is unassigned.

02 / Medium priority · medium cost

Cross-scale gravity–terrain step asymmetry

Layers and signature: isostatic gravity anomaly and detrended elevation; compare paired cross-strike gradients over several scales while requiring a persistent signed step in both. Buried basin-margin displacement may yield a broader gravity contrast than its subdued surface expression, potentially revealing a missing trace.

Non-fault mimics: an alluvial-fan edge, lithologic contact or erosional escarpment. Unlike this repo's existing documentation-only work, this is a paired signed profile; related gravity and relief methods exist in peer repositories, so novelty remains unproven. Only the described competition layers are proposed.

03 / Lower priority · medium cost

Seismicity-shape residual

Layers and signature: provided earthquake-density and gravity-slope layers; compare elongated earthquake-density residuals against a broad regional background, then test whether gravity boundaries align with the residual axis. A distributed seismic corridor may flag a weakly expressed structure not already in a catalogue.

Non-fault mimics: variable detection coverage, quarry activity or an unrelated intrusive swarm. Earthquake-density smoothing and event provenance may make localization too coarse; no raw-event timing is implied. This repo has not implemented the specific residual, but peer seismic methods are related.

04 / Conditional only · high cost

Radiometric across-contact reversals

Layers and signature: official GeoDAWN potassium and equivalent-thorium grids plus detrended elevation; compare across-boundary changes in the K/eTh contrast after correcting for terrain/cover, rather than treating a ratio high as a fault. An alteration or juxtaposition boundary may reveal a concealed structure.

Non-fault mimics: stratigraphic contacts, surficial deposits, moisture and survey artifacts. The USGS release lists GeoTIFF grids and a CC0 designation, but binary access and exact layers were not verified in this restricted runtime. This idea is not yet viable; peer radiometric fusion is related and novelty is unproven.

Scientific & competition sources

What is verified, and what it means

  • DrivenData problem and metric: fault labels, supplied features, triangular-kernel DTI, and submission format.
  • DrivenData staff clarification: known-fault mask is pixel-exact; new faults can occur near known traces.
  • USGS GeoDAWN release: magnetic/radiometric survey, acquisition geometry and data limitations.
  • USGS Granite Springs study and Battle Mountain study: geophysical interpretation of blind geothermal settings, not proof that our proposed detector works.
  • DOE GDR INGENIOUS data: publicly listed regional layers under CC BY 4.0; binary downloads not verified in this runtime.
  • USGS 3DEP: elevation products are listed as free; regional coverage and this runtime's download access still require checking.

The target is a geological fault map. A predicted fault does not establish an active geothermal vent, permeability, temperature, or commercial resource.
