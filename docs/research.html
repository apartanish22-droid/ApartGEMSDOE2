Research slate · untested

Four ideas. No
manufactured scores.

These are qualitative hypotheses, not assigned lanes or predictions. Their ranking is a planning judgment, and novelty beyond this checkout is not established.

These four hypotheses were not run in this review.

A separate parallel session ran a different, self-selected AP-1 idea and reported a negative result. Data access, lane assignment, registry completeness, the spent budget and the literal uniqueness rule block further testing here.

01 / Paired drainage offsets

Layers: 3DEP DEM, detrended elevation and isostatic gravity gradient. Signature: paired channel or terrace profiles displaced across a persistent topographic break, corroborated by a subsurface gradient. Concealed basin-margin displacements could persist away from mapped traces. Mimic: fluvial terrace erosion and alluvial-fan avulsion. Difference: paired opposite-side profiles, not a single ridge or stream-χ score; not implemented in this review. Cost: high; requires actual DEM tiles and coverage checks.

02 / Flight-line robustness

Layers: GeoDAWN magnetic grids plus individual magnetic flight-line measurements and position/altitude. Signature: require a candidate edge to survive independent leave-flight-line-out reconstructions at a common height. This may reduce acquisition artifacts while preserving concealed magnetic contacts. Mimic: lithological contacts without fault displacement. Difference: independent line-withholding stability, not only grid-level smoothing. Cost: high; published line data have not been downloaded here.

03 / Cross-depth conductance contrast

Layers: USGS Great Basin MT conductance maps for 2–12 km and 12–20 km, plus gravity gradient. Signature: a laterally persistent change in the ratio of depth-integrated conductances coincident with a basin margin. It may expose a concealed structural transition even where a surface catalogue is incomplete. Mimic: non-fault clay or salinity variation and inversion artifacts. Difference: explicit cross-depth contrast rather than a single conductance layer. Cost: medium–high; coarse resolution limits surface inference.

04 / Geochemical discontinuity

Layers: INGENIOUS well/spring chemistry and temperatures, DEM and gravity. Signature: cross-margin contrast in spatially blocked, uncertainty-weighted fluid chemistry rather than treating every spring as a fault. Mimic: lithology or groundwater mixing unrelated to displacement. Difference: paired chemical contrast, not point proximity. Cost: high; coverage, chemistry fields and licence must be checked for the actual area.

What is needed before testing?

Verify a method assignment, restore the original licensed data and cached stack, freeze a complete registry and a same-evaluator best, pre-register the experiment, and resolve the literal lattice issue without silently exempting it. Whole-component buffered validation, one-feature leakage canaries, both uniqueness stages and a separate selector remain mandatory.
