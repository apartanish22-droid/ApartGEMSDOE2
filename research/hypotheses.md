**Status: proposals only.** No assigned method paragraph was supplied and no experiment, surface, HOLDOUT-DTI, or TIFF was generated. Ranking reflects qualitative expected utility and implementation cost, not measured improvement. The checkout was initially empty, so all four are unimplemented here; we cannot establish that every related idea is novel across the many sister repositories. A feature available as a published download is not necessarily retrievable from this sandbox or suitable for the target grid. | 1 | **Paired drainage displacement.** USGS 3DEP bare-earth DEM, competition detrended elevation/slope and isostatic-gravity gradient; match opposing channel/terrace profiles across a persistent break and measure geometric displacement with uncertainties. | A degraded or buried basin-margin trace may offset drainage even if not in the surface catalogue; gravity is supporting context, not proof. | Fluvial terrace erosion, alluvial-fan avulsion, road cuts. | Unlike a single ridge detector or single stream-χ response (see [GEMSDOE36 TCD-36](https://buffedlizard55-lab.github.io/GEMSDOE36/docs/)), this tests paired cross-break profiles. **High cost.** [USGS 3DEP](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program) says products are free and without use restrictions; actual GeoDAWN tiles/coverage and bytes were not obtained here. **Not yet viable in this sandbox.** |
| 2 | **Independent flight-line stability.** GeoDAWN individual magnetic line data, actual flight location/height and gridded magnetic anomaly; leave flight lines out and retain gradient edges stable across independently regridded subsets at a common height. | A subsurface magnetic contrast can be visible beneath alluvium where catalogue coverage is incomplete. | Lithological contacts, volcanic unit boundaries, residual flight-line leveling. | Withholding independent acquisition lines is distinct from simply smoothing a grid or destriping. **High cost.** [USGS GeoDAWN](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and) explicitly lists line CSV, grids and flight paths and marks the release CC0. Exact files, fields and coverage were not downloaded. **Not yet viable in this sandbox.** |
| 3 | **Cross-depth conductance contrast.** USGS `gb_conductance_surface_tp.tif` (2–12 km) and `gb_conductance_middle_crust_tp.tif` (12–20 km), plus gravity gradient; test a stable lateral contrast of depth-integrated conductance ratios at a basin margin. | A buried structural transition may affect deep conductivity even without a mapped surface trace. | Conductive clay/salinity contrasts, lithological transitions and inversion artifacts. | This uses an explicit cross-depth contrast rather than one conductivity feature. **Medium–high cost** with severe scale mismatch. The [USGS ScienceBase release](https://doi.org/10.5066/P9TWT2LU) lists the files and depth ranges; their bytes, licence details and alignment were not checked in this sandbox. **Not yet viable.** |
| 4 | **Fluid-chemistry discontinuity.** INGENIOUS well/spring chemistry and temperature, DEM and gravity; compare uncertain chemical signatures across an inferred margin rather than count nearby hot springs. | Distinct fluid populations on opposite sides of a structural barrier might reveal an unmapped fault-related permeability contrast. | Lithological variation and groundwater mixing without fault displacement. | Paired chemistry contrasts differ from point-proximity features. **High cost.** The [GDR compilation](https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391) publicly lists well/spring chemistry and temperature with CC BY 4.0 attribution; local coverage, missingness, time confounding and actual bytes are unverified. **Not yet viable.** |

## Mandatory validation if an actual lane is assigned

Pre-register the mechanism and actual unique data products; confirm no catalogue leakage; build all label-dependent features from fold-visible faults only; withhold whole connected fault segments with a spatial buffer; mask visible catalogue pixels exactly; use the shared evaluator and writer; run standalone feature leakage canaries; compare pooled HOLDOUT-DTI and a paired spatial-block 95% CI with a frozen same-protocol best; check the complete registry before placement and on final dots; validate saved bytes against the authenticated original template; and use a separate automated selector. The public score of an unseen expert-mapped target cannot be inferred from a holdout ratio alone.

The [official about page](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/968/) describes faults as potential fluid conduits, not proof of active geothermal vents. [USGS cautions](https://pubs.usgs.gov/mf-maps/mf1136/mf1136/limit.htm) that non-fault processes can resemble scarps; [GeoDAWN](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and) documents variable flight height and survey processing. These support cautions, not the claimed performance of the proposals.
